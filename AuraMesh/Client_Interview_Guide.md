# Client Interview Guide — Barangay / DRRM Officer (Fact-Finding)
*(Use this interview with a prospective barangay officer or municipal DRRM officer to replace `[TO VALIDATE]` tags before your defense. No partner has been secured yet — this guide is for the first contact with a candidate partner.)*

> **Before the interview:** explain the project briefly, ask permission to take notes, and disclose that you are students with no client yet seeking a partner. Do not record or collect personal or case data without consent. All incident information should later be de-identified.

---

### Section 1: Role, Jurisdiction & Operations
1. **Role & Scope:** What is your role in the barangay / DRRM office, and how large is the area you cover (barangays, population, main streets)?
2. **Responder Structure:** Who responds to emergencies today — tanods, BDRRMC volunteers, barangay staff? Roughly how many are active?
3. **Current Tools:** What do you use today to coordinate a response (radio, group chat, megaphone, phone calls, a system)?

---

### Section 2: Current Search Workflow (As-Is)
4. **Trigger:** When a vulnerable person (a child, or an elderly person with dementia) is reported missing, what happens first, and who decides?
5. **Step-by-Step:** Walk us through the search from the moment it is reported to the moment it ends.
6. **Coordination:** How do you decide who searches where? Is there a map or shared plan, or is it decided informally?
7. **Sightings/Tips:** How do community members send in sightings or tips today (chat, calls, walk-ins)? How do you track them?
8. **False Alarms:** Do unverified or duplicate tips cause problems? How do you currently sort real leads from noise?

---

### Section 3: Volume, Timings & Pain Points
9. **Volume:** Roughly how many missing-person incidents do you handle per year, and of what kinds (children, elderly, others)? *(Confirm against de-identified records later.)*
10. **Early Hours:** In the first hour or two, what is typically the biggest difficulty — getting people out, deciding where to search, or handling the reports coming in?
11. **Coverage Gaps:** Do searchers ever overlap or leave areas uncovered? How would you know?
12. **Alert Fatigue:** When you broadcast widely, do uninvolved residents start ignoring the alerts? How do you handle that?
13. **Prior Attempts:** Has the barangay tried any app or system for this before? What happened?

---

### Section 4: Devices, Data & Readiness
14. **Devices:** Do your responders carry smartphones with mobile data? Android or iOS?
15. **Network:** Is mobile signal reliable across the area, or are there dead zones?
16. **Map Data:** Do you have any existing maps, GIS data, or address references for the barangay?
17. **Willingness:** Would your team be willing to use and be trained on a web dashboard (officers) and a mobile app (responders)?

---

### Section 5: Data Privacy & Legal
18. **Consent:** If a family reports a missing vulnerable person, is there a standard way to get consent to use their photo and details for the search?
19. **Minors:** Are there special procedures for handling children's information?
20. **Retention:** How long is incident information normally kept, and who can access it?
21. **DPA:** Does the office have a data protection officer or a policy we should follow under RA 10173?

---

### Section 6: Collaboration & Next Steps
22. **Partner Commitment:** Would the office consider formally endorsing the project as an industry partner?
23. **Data Access:** Could you provide de-identified records (counts, dates, timelines) to help us establish a baseline?
24. **Evaluation:** Would your team be available for a walkthrough or a simulated search drill for evaluation?
25. **Collision Check:** Do you know of any existing barangay DRRM or **hazard-mapping** system in the area that we should be aware of or avoid overlapping with?
26. **Contact:** Who should we follow up with, and what is the best way to reach them?

---

### Closing Script
*"Thank you. To be clear, we have no partner yet, and nothing you tell us becomes a claim until you confirm it in writing. Our next step would be an endorsement letter and, if you agree, a share of de-identified records and a simulated drill for evaluation. We will never process real case photos without a confirmed lawful basis and consent."*
