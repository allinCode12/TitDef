## 1. Postmortem: why a title can be AI-positive and still get rejected

What we know and what we infer:

| # | Factor | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No real client.** The stated rejection advice was "have a real client." | ✅ Known (stated) |
| 2 | **AI-default idea space.** Receipt/payment/e-wallet/POS topics are among the most frequently AI-suggested capstone ideas — panels see them constantly and pattern-match instantly. | Inferred, high confidence |
| 3 | **AI sycophancy loop.** AI tools validate, polish, and praise whatever they're given; they do not reject your idea the way a panel does. Every "positive" review was measuring *writing quality*, not *evidence*. | Inferred from your report |
| 4 | **Claim inflation** (fraud/fake-screenshot detection etc.) with zero client evidence — easy for a panel to attack. | Inferred from old pack |
| 5 | **No evidence access:** without a client, no interviews, no records, no baseline — the entire evidence-first method collapses at the first question. | Logical consequence of #1 |

**Rule for Title 3:** a title is not a sentence to be polished — it is the *output* of a real client + a real process + a real problem you can actually observe.

## 3. The AI-Smell Test (kill these before an advisor sees them)

**Automatic smells — title rewrite or reject:**
- "Smart …", "AI-Powered …", "Intelligent …", "Blockchain …", "Leveraging …" ( buzzword openings )
- Generic beneficiaries: "…for Small Businesses", "…for Students", "…for Everyone"
- Three+ capabilities stacked in one title (the whole point of Module 1: ONE transaction)
- A problem no one in the room has ever personally watched happen
- "Real-time … Monitoring Dashboard" as the entire value proposition
- Any title that works unchanged for a different school's capstone (if a stranger could submit it tomorrow with their school's name, it's not yours)

**Green signals:**
- A specific setting only your team can access ("…for a Party Service Business" works because *you have the party service business*)
- The capability names a **transaction** (request → confirm → fill; book → queue → serve)
- You can already picture who you'd interview first

**The smell test is necessary but not sufficient:** ResiboCheck probably passed the smell test for *you* and still died — which is why G1 (client) comes first.

### 3.1 Update 2026-10-08 — the prof-mandated AI rule (first consultation)

The first prof consultation required **AI, algorithms, and analytics in all three titles** ("should have AI integrated for ALL Titles… Algorithm, Analytics and AI included for all Titles"), and flagged the CrewSync title as *"seems small."* This **overrides the automatic buzzword ban for AI wording** — but only for *functional* AI. Decorative AI still fails G7. An AI component passes only if it has **all five**:

1. **One defined function** (what exactly does it predict/classify/rank?)
2. **Input data you actually possess** (client records, tracer studies, chat logs — not imagined datasets)
3. **An honest evaluation metric** (predicted-vs-actual, agreement-vs-manual-coding, before/after time)
4. **Human-in-the-loop for consequential decisions** (AI ranks/flags; a person confirms)
5. **One-term implementability** — rules first, sophistication grows with data (cold-start = pure rules)

**"AI" with no data, no metric, and no evaluator is still a G7 failure** — the ResiboCheck ghost wore a lab coat. Approved portfolio pattern: *transparent rules enforce the constraints; AI ranks/flags/predicts within those constraints; a human confirms.*

## 5. Candidate *shapes* for discussion — NOT recommendations

> These are conversation starters for your client inventory (§6). **None is approved. None should be submitted before clearing all 8 gates.** An advisor may prefer a different direction entirely — that is what the advisor meeting is for.

### Shape A — Service-shop scheduling (external business, like CrewSync's proven path)
- **Example shape:** "…Booking and Queue Coordination System for [a specific clinic/tutorial center/dental office]"
- **Transaction:** customer books → shop confirms slot → queue/status tracked
- **Client path:** relative's / family friend's shop (the path that already worked once)
- **WMA fit:** customers/staff mobile (away from counter) + owner web
- **Rejection risk:** medium — "appointment systems" are common in AI suggestion lists; survives only with a specific client + workflow detail
- **Overlap vs CrewSync:** medium (both are scheduling) — differentiate on *customer-facing booking* vs *internal staffing*, or discard if overlap feels too close

### Shape B — Community organization mobilization (least AI-typical)
- **Example shape:** "…Event Mobilization and Member Response System for [church ministry / sports league / barangay youth group / HOA]"
- **Transaction:** admin posts call/event → members respond before deadline → attendance/status tracked
- **Client path:** your own church, league, org, parents' HOA — whoever actually runs you
- **WMA fit:** members respond from phones; officers get a web dashboard
- **Rejection risk:** low-medium — rarely seen in AI default lists; honest small scope
- **Overlap vs CrewSync:** the transaction *feels* similar (request→respond→track) — be ready to articulate the difference (membership vs employment, recurring org vs per-event business) or pick another shape

### Shape C — Small delivery / field-team coordination (sector distance)
- **Example shape:** "…Delivery and Field Task Coordination System for [a family-run delivery/food/cargo business]"
- **Transaction:** dispatch assigns job → rider confirms/updates → status monitored
- **Client path:** relatives/neighbors in delivery, meal business, or cargo
- **WMA fit:** rider mobile + dispatcher web — textbook WMA justification
- **Rejection risk:** medium — "delivery tracking" appears in AI lists; needs client-specific detail (their actual dispatch method) to distinguish
- **Overlap vs CrewSync:** medium (dispatch = dispatch) — same differentiation duty as Shape B

### Explicitly in the AI-Default Zone (do not propose without a client begging for it)
Attendance systems (school/employee), inventory/POS/receipts (ResiboCheck's zone), grading/GPA analyzers, generic chatbots, "smart" anything, library systems, waste/laundry/booking templates with no client name attached.

## 6. The actual process (client-first, in order — do not skip steps)

1. **Client inventory** (½ hour, team brainstorm): list every person/office you can reach — family businesses, neighbors, parents' workplaces, community orgs, FEU offices, alumni businesses. Who is *already* asking for help with a process?
2. **Pain check** (10-min informal chat each): "Walk me through how you do X today." — listen, don't pitch. If nothing hurts → strike them off.
3. **Gate check** (this file, §2): which surviving client passes G1–G8?
4. **Title derivation** (Module 1 four elements, one transaction only)
5. **Advisor taste test:** present as an options sheet (§7) — advisors approve *from options*, not from desperation

## 6.1 Structured brainstorm protocol (run this week — inventory is currently thin)

**Standing inventory result (honest baseline):** family businesses ❌ · community orgs ❌ · FEU offices ⚠️ (already carrying AlumniTrack) · **friends' workplaces ✅ (mostly remote)** · general: none obvious yet.

### Round 1 — Mine your own week (each team member, 30 min, before any pitching)
Write down, per member:
1. **5 places any team member physically goes weekly** (church, gym, salon, clinic, canteen, barangay hall, cousin's store, delivery pickup point…)
2. **3 processes that personally annoyed someone** on the team (waiting, redoing forms, "sir, wala pa po yung reply", texting the same info twice)
3. **2 things a relative/friend has complained to you about** their work or business — verbatim, not summarized

**Rule:** only entries where a team member *witnessed the process firsthand*. Internet examples don't count.

### Round 2 — Complaint-mining questions (ask in casual conversation, 10 min, no pitching)
- Where does information in your work get **lost or delayed**?
- What do you **retype or copy-paste** between apps?
- What does someone have to **follow up on repeatedly**?
- What goes wrong when two people act on the **same information at once**?
- If you could delete one part of your week's admin work, what dies first?
- **Who would fix it if it were fixable?** *(this answer = your Gate 1 client)*

Strike off anyone who answers "it's fine, honestly" — a satisfied process is a dead end (the AlumniTrack rule applies to every candidate: *if the client says it works, report it works*).

### Remote-friend probe — ask these 6 before investing further
Remote workplaces are your one live lead, but they carry **two known traps**:

| Trap | Why it kills titles | Probe question |
|---|---|---|
| **Friend ≠ process owner** | G1 needs an office head who can endorse; a rank-and-file remote employee can't (ResiboCheck lesson, restated) | "Who in your company owns this process — and could we talk to them?" |
| **Tool saturation** | Distributed companies often already run Slack/Jira/Asana/Deel — pain may be low, and "replace our SaaS" is an unwinnable capstone | "What do you use for this today? Does it annoy you, or is it fine?" |

**Promising if their answers reveal:** coordination *between* a company tool and side channels (chat + spreadsheet + email for the same thing), handoffs across time zones with no status visibility, or paper/manual steps a remote-first company still keeps. **Dead end if:** "we already have a tool for that."

### Round 3 — Filter survivors through §2 (G1–G8) in one sitting
1. Cross off anyone without an owner contact (G1 fail).
2. Cross off anything in §3's AI-Default Zone (G7 fail).
3. Cross off anything whose core transaction duplicates CrewSync's (request→confirm→track) or AlumniTrack's (submit→consolidate→report) unless the difference is trivially explainable (G6 fail).
4. Whatever survives → 10-minute pain-check conversation → derive ONE candidate title from the surviving client's actual words.

**Deadline discipline:** brainstorm ends in 7 days. If Round 3 yields nothing, the fallback is not "invent a title" — it's widening Round 1 to relatives' and neighbors' workplaces (sources 1/4), because **a title without G1 cannot be submitted.**

## 10. CrewSync Option B — booking-inclusive variant (HELD, not canonical)

> **Status: on hold.** Not adopted as the canonical title. Promote to canonical ONLY if (a) the prof confirms "seems small" means scope, or (b) client fact-finding shows real booking pain (how customers book today, what breaks). Until then Option A (v2) stands.

**Why it's tempting:** a customer-facing booking portal visibly broadens the system — it reads "bigger" to a panel. **Why it's a trap:** it is a *second transaction* and a *third interface* (customers + owner + talents), it contradicts the declared guard *"customers are not users… book via existing channels,"* and it adds unvalidated requirements (does the owner even want to move bookings off Messenger/Facebook?) for no client evidence yet — the exact ResiboCheck pattern.

**If it happens, do it INTEGRATED, not bolted on:**

| Design decision | Option B choice |
|---|---|
| Title (Option B draft) | CrewSync: An AI-Assisted Web and Mobile-Based System for Event Booking, Talent Response Analytics, and Schedule Conflict Prevention for a Party Service Business |
| Transaction | Customer books (package + date) → owner confirms → system **auto-generates talent slots** → staffing workflow (unchanged) — ONE pipeline, not two systems |
| Customer interface | Responsive web (no install barrier), booking request → owner confirmation first (inquiry model) |
| Payments | Default **out of scope** unless the client explicitly asks (keeps economic feasibility sane) |
| Scope guards removed | "Customers book via existing channels" (replaced by the portable + booking interface) |
| Beneficiaries | Customers upgrade from indirect → direct users |
| What's kept | All staffing rules, AI scoring component, state machine unchanged (reused) |

**Two data points decide it — get both before any file sweep:**
1. **Prof:** ask the question in §9 (open question #4) at the next consultation.
2. **Client (fact-finding):** *"How do customers book today?"* and *"What actually goes wrong with booking?"* — if the owner says Messenger works fine, the portal is a feature nobody asked for; report that honestly.
